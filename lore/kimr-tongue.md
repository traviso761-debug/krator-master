# Kimr tog: the keepers' talk (draft, 2026-11-11)

The tongue of the **Kimr**, the people of the Labyrinth (`LORE.md` section 6.24, `biomes/labyrinth/PLAN.md` section 5). Every form
here is *proposed* unless marked: a draft from the owner's brief of 2026-11-11 ("conan-the-barbarian types... southwestern meets
cimmerian"), revised on the owner's answers of the same day (**the keepers' English, with loanwords to and from the Fellgangs,
most through the raiders**: section 7). What is still open is at the end. When the owner has settled them, the tongue gets its tab on
`lore/tongues-of-krator.html` (the family row, the phrasebook, a "From English" table), as every other tongue has.

## 1. The family: English, by the keepers' road (not Tungang) [the owner, 2026-11-11]

Every tongue in the crater is English worn down (`lore/tongues-of-krator.html`, "One word, twenty-seven roads"). The question is
which English, and the answer is **not the Tungangs'**.

- **The Tungangs** (`LORE.md` section 6.1) are the Rift line's crews: their English was the north of England's, kept by a union, and
  their Fellgang kin carried it onto the fells round the Labyrinth. That is why the Labyrinth's rivers carry Tungang names
  (*Siphon-Pit Beck*, *Graben-Tarn Gill*, the *Gowd Warra*): the scale model's namer gives the Labyrinth to the Tungang lexicon
  (`tools/scale-model/smodel/river_names.py`, `'Labyrinth': ('tungang', 2)`), and in the world they are **the Fellgangs' names**,
  the names of neighbours who saw the maze from its rims. The Kimr have their own (section 8).
- **The Kimr** come from the Hall of Heroes, the arcology at the Labyrinth's mouth (`biomes/labyrinth/PLAN-GATE.md`): the town of the
  preserve's **keepers**, not of tunnel crews. Their English was the keepers' working English: the speech of rangers, wardens, vets and
  stockmen on a ranch the size of a country, with its ranch words (*range*, *paddock*, *pen*, *count*, *boss*, *fall* for autumn,
  *y'all*). The people's own name is that job: **Kimr**, from *keepers* (section 3).
- **What they share with the Tungangs** is what all Krator English shares, and the words that crossed at Wildcat and the Firebliss
  camps (section 7). Kimr tog keeps no *t'*, no *nobbut*, no *-na*. A Kimr and a Fellgang who are not kin understand each other's
  names for animals, the loans, and little else; at Wildcat, where the two peoples have married for generations, both tongues
  are spoken at one fire (`LORE.md` section 6.24, "Relations").

**The ear it was worn to.** The brief's two halves are given to the two halves of the tongue:
- **The Cimmerian half, the sounds:** Welsh, the speech of the *Cymry*, whose name *Kimr* happens to echo (Howard's Cimmerians took
  theirs from the same root). Short, heavy words; *ll*, *dd*, *ff*; *w* and *y* as vowels; consonants that soften at the start of a
  word (the mutation, section 4).
- **The Southwestern half, the grammar and the speech-ways:** Hopi and the Keresan and Zuni pueblos. Plurals of animals by doubling
  (a herd is a word said twice), a glottal catch, the dead spoken of in a form of their own, and a calendar of masked visitors.

A Welsh speaker would hear something familiar in its sounds and understand nothing; a Hopi speaker would know the shape of its
year and none of its words.

## 2. Sounds and spelling

Kimr tog writes with Welsh habits on the Latin letters (no Kimr writes: section 9). It uses **k**, never *c*, because the owner
spells the people *Kimr*.

**Vowels:** *a e i o* as in Welsh or Spanish; **w** is the vowel of *pool* (*bwl*, "bull"); **y** is the vowel of *the* (*melyn*,
"melon"). A doubled vowel is long (*ii* in *kiib*, "keep"). Diphthongs: **ai** (*fire*), **ei** (*day*), **aw** (*out*), **ow**
(*old*), **iw** (*you*).

**Consonants:**

| Letters | Sound | As in |
|---|---|---|
| p b t d k g, m n, l r, s, h, w, i (before a vowel: y) | as in English | |
| **ff** | f | *ffair*, fire |
| **f** | v | *Fer*, the bear's boldness |
| **th** | th in *thin* | *Lloth*, sloth |
| **dd** | th in *this* | *mwddr*, mother |
| **ll** | the Welsh *ll*: a breathed l, the tongue set for l and the air blown past it | *llan*, clan |
| **rh** | a breathed r | *Rhainw*, woolly rhino |
| **ng** | ng in *sing*, also at the start of a word | |
| **ch** | ch in *church* | *chaild*, child |
| **sh** | sh | *shild*, shield |
| **j** | j in *judge* | *Reinj*, the Range |
| **ʼ** | a catch in the throat (glottal stop) | *mawʼn*, mountain |

**Stress** falls on the first syllable of every word; a compound keeps the stress of its first part.

**Homophones the changes made** (the Kimr play on them): *swn* is both *sun* and *son*; *wer* is *wear* and *where*; *gwd* is *good*
and *gut*; *marw*, marrow, sounds like nothing in particular to a Kimr, and like *dead* to a Welsh ear.

## 4. A grammar sketch

**Verb first.** A clause runs verb, subject, object (Welsh order): *Di kil Krag Awrog*, "Krag killed an aurochs" (PAST kill Krag
aurochs). It came out of English questions and *did*-sentences (*did Krag kill...*), which the keepers' descendants made the plain
form. The Kimr say a statement is spoken as if to the dead, who are asked to witness it.

**Tense** is a particle before the verb: **di** past (*did*), **wil** future (*will*); a bare verb is present or timeless (proverbs,
law). **na** before the verb is *not*; **na** alone is *no*, **ia** is *yes*.

**The taking mutation.** The grammar's heart, and the culture's (section 6.24 of `LORE.md`, "Virtue-taking"). A noun whose
**virtue is held by someone** changes its first sound, as Welsh nouns soften after certain words:

| Plain | Taken | | Plain | Taken |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p | b | | m | f |
| t | d | | ll | l |
| k | g | | rh | r |
| b | f | | s | h |
| d | dd | | a vowel | h + the vowel |
| g | (dropped) | | | |

- **Awrog** is the great aurochs, the animal; **Hawrog** is the aurochs *as held*: kingship, and the king who holds it.
- **Twsgr** is a tusker, any pachyderm; **Dwsgr** is its toughness, and a warrior in tusker hide.
- It is used: after the verbs of taking (**teig** take, **wer** wear, **iid** eat, **peint** paint, **kari** carry): *Wer hi Dwsgr*,
  "he wears the tusker's toughness" (wears he Tusker-taken); after a person's name, for what that person has taken: *Krag Hawrog*,
  "Krag who holds the Aurochs", the war-king; and for a hero carried by a masked dancer (section 5).
- A Kimr can say *wer hi twsgr haid*, "he wears tusker hide", a plain fact about leather; or *wer hi Dwsgr*, a claim about the man.
  Saying the second of someone who has not earned it is an insult, and a challenge.

**Number.** Things and people take no plural (*thri sbir*, three spears; *pibl*, people). **Animals double for a herd**, the
first syllable said twice (Hopi's reduplication): *bwl* a bull, *bwbwl* a herd of bulls; *Mamth* a mammoth, *Mamamth* a mammoth
herd; *Elg*, *Elelg*. A Kimr hears the herd in the doubling, the way a river is heard in a long word.

**The dead are always plural.** A dead person, even one, is **ddei**, "they" (English *they*), never *hi*: a hero lives on in all
who carry them. *Na dai Hirw: kari wi ddei.* "Heroes do not die: we carry them." Calling a living person *ddei* wishes them dead;
calling a dead hero *hi* says no one carries them, and is the worst thing one can say at a funeral.

**Pronouns:** **ai** I; **iw** you (one); **hi** he, **shi** she; **wi** we; **iwl** you (many: the keepers' *y'all*); **ddei** they,
and every dead person.

**Possession and modifiers follow the noun** (Welsh order): *horn Awrog*, the aurochs's horn; *Mamth wwli*, the woolly mammoth;
*Hol Hirw*, the hall of heroes. The article is **y**, before every noun: *y Reinj*, the Range.

**Sentences to keep:**

| Kimr | Word by word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Gwd hwnt.* | good hunt | the greeting |
| *Kiib y Reinj.* | keep the Range | the farewell; also the oath |
| *Di kil Krag Awrog.* | PAST kill Krag aurochs | Krag killed an aurochs |
| *Wer hi Dwsgr.* | wears he Tusker-taken | he wears the tusker's toughness |
| *Na dai Hirw: kari wi ddei.* | not die heroes: carry we them | heroes do not die: we carry them |
| *Go awd, kwm in.* | go out, come in | said over the dead: go out (by the river) and come in (in the masks) |

## 5. Names

**Personal names come in three.**
1. **The small name**, given at birth: an English word worn short, often a thing seen near the birth: *Krag* (crag), *Darg* (dark),
   *Sdorm* (storm), *Llint* (flint), *Ffern* (fern), *Byrn* (burn), *Kol* (coal), *Ros* (rose), *Wynd* (wind), *Ais* (ice), *Grim*,
   *Bold*, *Hyrd* (herd), *Sdil* (still).
2. **The taken names**, added one at a time, each the taken form of an animal (or a hero) whose virtue the person has claimed by a
   kill or a gift: *Krag Hemr* (Krag of the sabre-tooth's ferocity), *Ffern Helg* (Ffern of the elk's swiftness), *Darg Dwsgr Ddair*
   (Darg tusker-tough and wolf-true). A Kimr's name is a list of deeds: it grows through life, and a herald (section 6.24's Teller)
   can read a stranger's life from it.
3. **The hero's name**: the dead who are made heroes are called **Hirw** before the name, take the plural pronoun, and keep every
   taken name: *Hirw Krag Hawrog Dwsgr*. The oldest heroes have no small names at all, only the keepers' job titles (section 6.24,
   "The First Keepers"): *y Reinjr* (the Ranger), *y Wordn* (the Warden), *y Fed* (the Vet), *y Kawntr* (the Counter).

**Clans** are **llan** (clan) and take the name of a founding hero or animal: *Llan Elg*, *Llan Krag*, *Llan Lloth*. A person says
their clan after their names: *Ffern Helg, Llan Elg*.

**Villages** are **tawn** (town) and the place: *Tawn Geid*, the gate village; *Tawn Teibl*, the mesa village.

**Animal virtues** are the animals' names in the taking mutation (section 4, and the glossary's third column).

## 8. Two names for every place

The Labyrinth's names on the map are the Fellgangs' (and the scale model's), in Tungang: the *Gowd Warra*, *Siphon-Pit Beck*,
*Graben-Tarn Gill*. The Kimr's names are their own and say what a place is for: the Gowd Warra is *y Wei Awd*, the Way Out, the one
river out of the Range, down which the dead are said to go (*Go awd, kwm in*). A kit that labels the Labyrinth for a player inside it
uses the Kimr names; the map and a Fellgang use the Tungang ones.

## 9. Writing

**The Kimr write nothing.** They paint **signs**, as the pueblos painted clan symbols: every hero has a sign (a figure on their mask's
brow, on their doll and on their niche in the Hall), every clan its animal, and the Count is kept as rows of tally strokes on the
Lookout's walls and on hide. The keepers' letters survive only as the Hall's stencilled Ancient signs, which the Kimr read as
holy figures and never as words.
