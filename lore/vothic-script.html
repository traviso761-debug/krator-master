Tzintzuntzan · the script of the Realm of the Son of the Sun

Vothic Script

Thirty-three letters, one for each sound of the tongue spoken from Shim to Zey'danin. The bowls and loops come from a Georgian hand, the blades and hooked serifs from Daedric. Vowels are round. Consonants end in a cut. A gold thorn marks a breathed sound.

Scribe

Digraphs are single letters: tzh chh tz ch ph th kh sh hl dh ng rh. A doubled vowel is a long one and is written twice. An apostrophe is the syllable cut; a full stop is the diamond. Latin c f j q w x have no letter.

The thirty-three

In the order of the English letters they descend from, as the Daedric list keeps the Latin order. Each letter is named for a word that begins with it, and every name is an English word worn down by the sound changes below. Click a letter to write it in the scribe.

Gold strokes are the added marks: the thorn (breath), the bar, the root (tail).

Marks

Most letters are their own shapes, as in Georgian. Three marks are systematic, so a reader who knows the plain letter can read its marked partner.

The thorn (Thon) is the breath mark. It turns a stop into its aspirated pair, as Purépecha pairs p/ph, t/th, k/kh, ts/tsh, ch/chh. It also sits on hl, the breathed l of Dunmer names like Hlaalu.

The bar crosses the ascender of d to make dh, much as Old English crossed its d to write ð.

The root is a tail below the line for sounds made at the back or the roof of the mouth: ng from n, retroflex rh from the flap r.

From English

The tongue descends from English across a long dark age, worn into a sound inventory between Purépecha and Dunmer: aspirated stops and affricates, a high central vowel, a retroflex r, a breathed l, and a fondness for v and dh. These are the regular changes. The letter names follow them.

Names on the record

Place and title names already in the lore, written out. -tzan "realm, place of" has no English source: it is a Chichani word that survived the conquest, and it echoes the real Purépecha town of Tzintzuntzan.
