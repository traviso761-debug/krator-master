## 1. Bookshelves
It is a library and you cannot see a single book. Put reading rooms behind the loggia and the lobed windows with
real shelving: ranges of tall cases against the walls and free-standing stacks between the branching columns, with
books as banded rows (a row of thin boxes in varied warm colours reads as books at any distance that matters), plus
reading desks, a lectern or two, ladders against the tall cases, and lamps. Make sure it is VISIBLE — through the
lobed windows, through the portal, and from the court — because an interior nobody can see is wasted. If that means
opening the front more, open it.

## 2. More covered roof area
Your own report says the branching court columns are only visible in plan or from the portal, which means the great
space reads as an open yard rather than a hall. Roof most of it: carry the undulating scaled roof over the reading
hall so the building reads as a covered volume, with clerestory light along the ridge (a band of small lobed windows
or a glazed strip under the wave) so the interior is still lit and still visible from outside. Keep the roof's
colour drift and the mosaic spine — that is the best thing about the building.

## 3. Cloisters outside
Wrap the building in an open cloister: a parabolic arcade (`F.arcade`) running along at least two sides, ideally
three, on bone columns matching the loggia's, with a shaded walk behind it, a mosaic-paved floor, benches, and
planting between the piers (`F.tree`). This is the one place in Yuni where the Gaudí and the Sahelian halves of the
city should meet, so let the cloister be the quiet, repetitive, shaded frame around the loud trencadis front.
The footprint may grow to about 64 x 52 to hold it — declare it honestly and keep everything inside.
