# The Catch: a closed basin (plan)

The owner's polygon `the catch` (2026-10-23; `regions` doc `rmvka7icdn1dq`; the older geographic region
`rmuf346nvpktx`) on the Krator Scale Model: a basin south-east of the Godthrone, walled on every side. The owner asked
(2026-10-23) whether even the rain it has now is generous. **It is, by about ten times on the floor.** This plan says why,
what the model should carry instead (a 4.22 item), and what kit the corrected basin wants. Everything is *proposed*
unless marked.

**The owner's brief** (2026-10-23): the Catch is **the biggest barrier to trade between Xanadu and everywhere else**. It
has oases, but **only just enough to make the crossing doable, not comfortable or safe**. The Ancients' tunnel to Stair
has been **closed by seismic activity since about the Cataclysm**. **Stair** is the nearest thing to a settlement: a
glorified campground, home to a handful of merchants, to the caravans passing through, and now and then to bands of
daring or desperate arcology divers. Its soft rock is **wind-carved lake beds**, not tuff.

## 2. Why the rain is generous

Rain needs water vapour, and vapour has to come in over the walls. What decides how much air can carry over a pass is the
**cold** there, not the pressure. Krator's dense air (1.6 atm at sea level, a scale height ~8 km) makes 6-7 km passes
breathable, 0.64-0.76 atm, like a 2.5-3.5 km pass on Earth. But the temperature still falls about 5-6 °C a kilometre,
so a 7 km pass is ~35-40 °C colder than the floor. Air at -20 °C and 0.65 atm holds ~1 g of water per kg; air on the floor
(+19 °C, 1.55 atm) holds ~9. Whatever crosses the walls is wrung to a tenth of what the floor's air could carry, has
dropped its rain on the outside slopes, and then sinks into the basin, warming and drying as it falls (a foehn). The
basin has **no lake and no sea** to supply vapour from inside.

The 4.21 figures most likely come from `reclimate`'s climate twins, as Pinnacle Rock's knoll did (`settlements/pinnacle-rock/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`):
the floor's twins are low, warm cells elsewhere on the crater, which are wet because they are open to the Ring Sea's air.
The climate pass does not see that the Catch is sealed.

**Earth's closed basins**, all more open than the Catch:

| Basin | Walls | Its lowest opening | Floor rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tarim (the Taklamakan) | 5-7 km | the east, open at ~1.2 km to the Gansu corridor; passes ~3.5-4 km on the west | 10-60 mm (Ruoqiang ~28, Hotan ~35, Kashgar on the rim ~65) |
| Turpan Depression | 4-5 km (the Bogda Shan) | open east and south at ~1 km | ~16 mm |
| Qaidam | 5-6 km | ~3.5 km | 15-50 mm in the west, ~150 in the east |
| Death Valley | ~3 km | many | ~50 mm |

The Catch has **no low side at all**, and its lowest notch is higher than any of these basins' passes. On Earth's numbers
it should be at least as dry as the Taklamakan's core.

## 3. What it should carry (the 4.22 correction)

| Band | 4.21 | Target | Class |

| piedmont, 1-3 km | 482 mm | **40-120 mm**, rising with height | BWk, BWh |
| inner walls, 3-5.5 km | 147 mm | **100-250 mm**, most of it snow: the walls wring out what crosses the passes, and the oases' and the terminal lake's own vapour rises up them on summer afternoons | BSk, Dsc and ET dry |

**Temperature.** Drier air means clear skies and a wider swing: the floor's warmest month **+33-35 °C** (from +29), the
coldest **+5-6 °C** (from +9), the daily range ~20 °C. The floor's air is dense (~1.55 atm), so the heat feels heavier
than Turpan's.

**Wind.** At night cold air drains off walls 10-17 km high, and the floor gets strong katabatic winds at dawn and
dust storms (the Tarim's *karaburan*, "black storm") in spring. Dense air moves more sand at the same wind speed, so the
dunes are big and the yardangs deep.

This goes on the model's to-do list as its own item (`tools/scale-model/README.md`, "4.22: the Catch, a closed basin").

## 4. Water

A dry basin can still have water, a little. Snow on the inner walls melts into short rivers, which run out onto the gravel
fans and sink into them; the last of the water pools in a salt lake at the lowest point. The owner wants **just enough**
(2026-10-23), so the plan keeps it scarce:

- **Most of the walls' snow never reaches the floor.** In air this dry and thin it sublimates where it falls. *Proposed*:
  ~10% runs off, ~1 km³ a year, ~30 m³/s for the whole basin, a few times what one small Tarim river carries.
- **The rivers die on the fans.** Only a handful of them, the biggest off the highest inner walls, surface again at the
  fans' toes, and those few springs and seeps are the **oases**: a pool, a reed bed, a stand of poplar and tamarisk, a
  well. **Five of them** on the crossing [the owner, 2026-10-23], none named yet. The crossing from the notch to the
  southern passes is ~700 km, so they lie *~100-150 km apart*: three to five days with loaded beasts, which carry water
  between them. A caravan that misses one, or finds it dry, is in trouble. No oasis waters fields, and **none is lived in
  but Stair's** [the owner].
- **The terminal lake** at the lowest point (px 762,1067, 225 m) is **salt, not drinking water**: a few hundred km² of
  brine and salt crust that moves as its feeders shift, the way Lop Nur did ("the wandering lake").
- **Groundwater** runs under the fans toward the floor. Wells dug at the fans' toes reach it; whether anyone ever dug
  tunnels to bring it out by gravity (Turpan's *karez*) is open, and the owner's "only just enough" says no one has done
  it at scale.

## 5. The landscape: stations

1. **The sand sea**: the floor's core, a Taklamakan erg with star and linear dunes up to a few hundred metres, wind-cut
   yardangs on old lake beds, gravel *reg* between. Nothing grows on the dunes but a few deep-rooted shrubs.
2. **The oases**: five springs and seeps at the fans' toes, days apart (section 4). Each is small: a pool
   or a well, a reed bed, a stand of riparian trees (*tugai*: Euphrates poplar, tamarisk analogues), trampled ground and
   old fire rings round it. **Stair**, the ruined arcology at 1,130 m, stands at the biggest.
3. **The terminal lake and the lake beds**: the salt lake and its crust, the dead channels where it used to lie, and the
   **wind-carved lake beds** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: old lake sediments cut by the wind into yardangs, ridges and
   castellated mesas, Lop Nur's "White Dragon Mounds".
4. **The fans and the walls**: gravel fans tens of kilometres long, the dry gorges the rivers come out of, the walls'
   snowfields and small glaciers above, and nothing at all above ~9 km. The view is the point: 10-17 km walls on every
   horizon, the Godthrone's mass to the north-west.

The flora should **reuse the eastern desert kit's species where they fit** (`biomes/sedesert`) and add only what a
riparian, salty, enclosed basin needs: a tugai tree, a salt cedar, a dune shrub, a reed. Which of the kit's
species fit has not been checked yet.

## 6. People and roads

- **The barrier** [the owner, 2026-10-23]. The Catch is the biggest barrier to trade between Xanadu and everywhere else,
  for two reasons: its distances and its water, and **the Stalkers** (section 6c).
  Most of what goes between them goes by caravan across it. That is why Xanadu's goods are dear and why the lore's merchants "from the crater to
  Xanadu through the Bowl and the Catch" (`LORE.md` §4, Hub 01) are rare and well paid.
- **The crossing.** In by the 6.2 km notch on the Bowl's side (NNW, near px 726,896), across the floor from oasis to
  oasis, out by the passes of ~7.3 km to the south and south-west, toward Strongbow (px 754,1264). The eastern passes
  lead into the Stalker Peaks, and nobody takes them (section 6c). At 0.64-0.76 atm the passes are breathable but
  bitterly cold, and the floor between them is a furnace. The hazards: **the Stalkers** above all (section 6c), the black
  storms and their caustic dust, an oasis found dry, the beasts' water running out between stages.
- **The Ancients' road** to Stair (`openworld/ANCIENT-ROADS.md`: Arcbeam to Stair) ran through a tunnel under the walls,
  **closed by seismic activity since about the time of the Cataclysm** [the owner, 2026-10-23]. It is **sealed halfway
  through**: both halves are open from their own ends, and both offer salvage. **Yuni stripped the northern half bare a
  long time ago**; the **southern half**, entered from Stair, is the divers' prospect.
- **Stair** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: the closest thing to a settlement in the Catch, and more of a **glorified
  campground**. Its ruin is **the Hill arcology** (`kits/ancients/targets/hill`, `src/89e-hill.js`: a city of 111
  garden terraces climbing ~500 m of hillside, sinuous along the contour), at the biggest of the five oases, and the only one lived in, by **a couple of hundred
  people**. A handful of merchants (who sell water, fodder, repairs and news at the prices the Catch allows), the
  caravans passing through, and now and then **bands of daring or desperate arcology divers** working the ruin.
- **Who** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: the residents, the traders and the oases' future names are **Yuni, Izani, Dasrī or
  Xanadui**, the four peoples whose roads meet here, with very occasional bold ventures from further off. Each oasis
  carries a name in more than one of their tongues, as Silk Road towns did (Kashgar, Kashi, Shule) [the owner].
- **Nobody else lives here.** No oasis people, no ruler of the passes.

## 6a. What the Catch has that nowhere else does

The owner asked (2026-10-23) what the divers bring out, and whether the Catch has anything, geologically or chemically,
that the rest of the planet does not. Two things follow from what it is: **the most sealed basin on Krator**, evaporating
since long before the Ancients, with no outlet at all, its walls the Godthrone's volcanic rock.

1. **The salts.** A closed basin keeps every salt its rivers ever carried in; only the water leaves. Where the catchment is
   volcanic rock, the brine goes **alkaline**: a **soda lake**, carbonate-rich, like Lake Natron and Mono Lake, with
   sulfate and chloride crusts where it dries (Lop Nur's potash). The Catch is **Krator's one great salt field**. For the
   game it comes down to **two resources** [the owner, 2026-10-23: no finer than that]: **potash** and **alkali salts**
   (soda and its kin; borates and lithium go in this one, not apart). Elsewhere on Krator they are traces.
   - **The dust is caustic.** Alkali dust off the dry lake beds, lifted by the black storms, burns the eyes and the
     lungs (Owens Lake's dust is the worst in the United States). One more reason the crossing is not safe.
   - **What they are good for**: potash for fields and for powder; alkali salts for glass, soap, glazes and flux, and
     for whoever rebuilds the Ancients' energy cells.
   - **The nodes are added when the kit is built** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: `kits/resources` gains a potash and an
     alkali-salt node then (its nodes now: iron, copper, gold, tin, silver, rare earth, coal, sulfur, saltpeter, oil;
     noted in its `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`).
2. **The preservation.** Rust needs water. In air this dry, sealed off for a thousand years, **Ancient metal and Ancient
   machines keep** as they do nowhere else on Krator: the Tarim's mummies and Turpan's silk lasted for the same reason.
   *Proposed*: that is the divers' trade. Out of Stair's lower levels and the tunnel's southern half they bring **alloys**
   (structural stock and fittings that come out bright, not as rust) and **energy cells**, intact and some still holding
   a charge, which in a wet ruin anywhere else would long since have corroded through. The caustic dust is the one
   enemy of the haul: what lay open to it is etched, what lay sealed is pristine.

Together: the Catch's salts are what a cell-maker needs, and its ruin is where the cells are. *Proposed*: Stair's merchants
buy both, and the buyers are the people who can use them: the Yusar (the Eternal State's cybernetics tribe, §6.19),
Yuni's and Locus's Geomancers, Xanadu's engine-keepers, the Empire's armourers.

## 6b. Stair's site

The Hill wants a hill: ~560 m of slope from its foot to its summit deck, ~1 km of plan, with the oasis at its foot. On the
model (2 km a pixel) the Catch has one hilly country to give it: **a range of interior hills across the basin's middle**
(px ~730-870 by 1038-1135), standing 1.5-2 km over the floor, to ~2.5 km. Stair's point today (px 827,1113, 1,130 m)
already lies among them, in **a hollow through the range** (~800-1,100 m) with ridges to the west (to ~1.7 km) and to the
east (rising from ~900 m to 1.7-2.1 km within 8-12 km).

That is Turpan's arrangement: the groundwater moving under the fans toward the floor is **dammed by the interior hills
and rises as springs** in the gaps through them, as it does at the Flaming Mountains, so the hollow is where the biggest
oasis belongs, and it lies on the crossing (the NNW notch to the southern passes). **Proposed site: the hollow's east
flank, px ~834,1112** (~1,000 m), the Hill climbing the slope eastward from the spring hollow and facing west over it.
The point moves ~14 km east of where it stands (a record edit on the model, `tools/scale-model/README.md`). The kit's own
landform (`89e-hill.js`, "the landform and the route") makes the slope at full scale; the model only has to put the hill
there, and it does.

## 6c. The Stalker Peaks and the Stalkers

[The owner, 2026-10-23, unless marked.]

**The Stalker Peaks** lie **east of the Catch**, named for the crossing's other great danger. On the model (4.21) they are
the ranges between the Catch's eastern wall and Hub 01: a first range of 7-11 km straight east of the basin (px ~960-1030),
then a high valley at ~4.4-6 km where **Hub 01** stands (ruined arcology, px 1072,1079, 4,417 m: mean -2 °C, ~50 mm), and
round that valley peaks of **13-15 km** (px 1015,963, 15.1 km; 1049,1151, 14.0; 1125,1175, 14.9; 1139,1079, 13.7). They
are not yet a region on the model (a record edit, `tools/scale-model/README.md`).

**The Stalkers** are **quiet robotic killers that stalk the sands**. They hunt down **straggling caravaneers**, or **lie in
wait for ambush under the sand**, and they work in **pairs or triads**. They are **about human size** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: small enough to
lie under a hand's depth of sand, big enough to carry a person off. They come in **two variants** [the owner]:
- **the harpooner**, with a **pneumatic harpoon gun**: it strikes from range, quietly (a hiss, no flash), and pins or drags
  its quarry;
- **the blade**, with **blade-arms** for close quarters.

*Proposed*: a pair is a harpooner and a blade, a triad one harpooner and two blades or the reverse: the harpoon takes a
straggler off its feet or a beast out of the line, and the blades finish it before the caravan can turn. They are why a caravan keeps together, why no one
walks out of sight of the fires at an oasis, and why the Catch's crossing is not safe at any price.

**Where they come from: the author's secret.** Their source is **Hub 01**, a ruined arcology **run by a mad AI**. In the
world **no one knows this**: no human has reached Hub 01 since the Cataclysm and lived to tell the tale. What the
caravaneers say is only that the Stalkers come out of the peaks to the east and go back into them, which is how the peaks
got their name. *Proposed*: the game shows it, never says it. A Stalker that is followed goes east; the Ancient road from
Hub 01 to Yuni (`openworld/ANCIENT-ROADS.md`, 44 km of it in tunnel) is the one way in, and no one who has taken it has come
back; a diver's salvage now and then carries Hub 01's mark.

**How it went mad** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: Hub 01 was **driven mad during the Cataclysm**, and **no one is sure how**:
the Ancients' contamination (the one that turned their robots on their makers, `LORE.md` §6.19), some kind of virus, or
deliberate reprogramming. The author leaves it open too; whatever the game shows should fit all three. *Proposed*: an
Heir who reached it, and used the Call, would learn which, and would be either its master or its next subject.

*Proposed* for the builds:
- **Quiet** means no tracks that last (the wind fills them), no light, little sound: a Stalker is seen when it moves, and
  it moves at the end.
- **In pairs or triads**: the harpooner stays back and shows nothing; the blades close. Under the sand, a triad lies along
  a caravan's line, a stage out from an oasis, the harpooner furthest out.
- **Stragglers**: they take the last beast, the sick, the one who went to relieve themself; they rarely take on a
  whole caravan.
- **Built** (2026-10-23): `kits/mechs`, culture `stalker`: `st_harpooner` and `st_blade` (`kits/mechs/README.md`),
  sleeker than the Iziz walkers, as the owner asked. Not yet: a buried pose and the harpoon's line
  (`kits/mechs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md`).

**Their look** [the owner's references, 2026-10-23]:
- **The harpooner** is the canvas-legged walker of the owner's reference (the one the Speculator was drawn from): a smooth
  white hull leaning forward on two legs in flexible sleeves, a round sensor head at the front, two thin manipulator arms
  hung under it, and **a harpoon gun on each side** of the hull.
- **The blade** is the four-armed heavy of the owner's reference (the Baiulus's): great white shoulders swelling over an
  open machine core, two small arms folded at the chest, squat bowed legs on splayed feet, and **hooked blades on the ends
  of the big arms** in place of hands.

**How a caravan fights them** [the owner, 2026-10-23]: a wise caravan **carries explosives, or more exotic Ancient
weapons**, to fight them; one that does not **flees**.

## 7. The lake beds (in place of tuff)

The tuff candidate (`biomes/WORLD.md`) assumed the Godthrone's ash, and the survey did not help it: the two vents near the
Catch's south-west notch (px 716,1128 and 770,1168) stand at 10.7 and 8.4 km on the airless walls, and the floor is
300-600 km from the Godthrone's summit. **The owner chose the wind-carved lake beds** (2026-10-23): the basin's soft rock is
old lake sediment, from wetter ages when the terminal lake was far bigger, cut by the wind into yardangs and castellated
mesas. Shelter for a caravan in a storm, and nowhere to live.
