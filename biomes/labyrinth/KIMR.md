# The Kimr: art and material style guide (draft, 2026-11-11)

"Southwestern meets Cimmerian" (the owner, 2026-11-11), written as rules for the kits that will draw the Kimr: the pueblos (a culture
in `kits/catalog` plus a building kit, placed with `core/hamlet`: `PLAN.md` section 5), the Hall of Heroes' funerary dressing
(`kits/ancients` targets/gatearc, `GATEARC.kimr`), their characters' dress (`core/charactermodels`) and their camps in the Firebliss
Range (`core/camp`). The culture is `LORE.md` section 6.24; the tongue, and every Kimr word below, is `lore/kimr-tongue.md`.

**Where this lives.** Here, with the biome that will place the Kimr: it is the design sheet of **`kits/kimr`**, the culture kit started
2026-11-11 with its first def, the example house (`kits/kimr/README.md`).

## 1. The two halves, and what each gives

| | Southwestern | Cimmerian |
|---|---|---|
| sources | Hopi, Zuni and Acoma pueblos; Ancestral Puebloan (Mesa Verde, Chaco: T-doors, towers, round kivas); Mimbres black-on-white pottery; katsina masks and dolls; Pueblo rock art | Robert E. Howard's Cimmerians as Frazetta and the 1982 film drew them: horned helms, furs over bare skin, scars, heavy weapons; the European Ice Age (mammoth-bone huts of Mezhyrich, cave painting) |
| gives | **the forms**: stepped room blocks, ladders, roof hatches, round rooms, flat earth colours, painted geometry, masks and dolls, the calendar | **the weight**: massive, rough, battered, horned; hide and fur and bone in bulk; nothing delicate except the painting |
| never | adobe prettiness: no tourist-pueblo smoothness, no turquoise on everything | no metal plate armour, no Viking ships, no Norse or Celtic knotwork (the Talovolci have the Celtic ornament: §6.21) |

**Krator's own rule comes first** (`LORE.md` section 5, "Moebius and Jodorowsky's Dune are the look"): silhouette first, flat clean
colour in large calm fields with one strong accent, organic and baroque (bone, horn, shell, grown things), nothing mass-produced.
The Kimr are the most literal case of it: their every object is part of an animal.

## 3. Rules by kind

### Architecture: the pueblo

1. **On a rock.** Every village stands on a knob, a mesa bench or a built plinth **6-15 m** over the floor (`PLAN.md` section 5). A
   **great house** (*greidhaws*: `LORE.md` 6.24) is the same at the scale of several hundred people, its blocks round a court
   on a mesa bench, the lesser villages on the knobs about it. The
   plinth's face is **battered** (sloped 10-15°) dry-laid chaos rock, its stones largest at the foot, cyclopean, never coursed neatly.
2. **Stepped room blocks**: 2-5 storeys of **2.4-2.8 m**, each set back **3-4 m** from the one below, so every storey has a roof
   terrace. Walls are rough stone below and **adobe or dung-straw plaster** above, the corners rounded by hand.
3. **No door at the ground.** The way in is by **ladders** to the first terrace and **hatches** in the roofs. A ladder's two poles
   stand **1.5-2 m above the hatch** (the pueblo silhouette) and are **capped with horn**. Ladders are drawn up at night: a kit draws
   them raised (night, a herd running) and down (day).
4. **T-shaped doorways** on the upper storeys only (after Mesa Verde), 1.6 m high, the crossbar at shoulder height, closed by a hide
   screen.
5. **Roof beams** (*vigas*): larch or poplar poles from the gallery woods, and **mammoth long bones** in the richer houses; their ends
   stand **0.3-0.6 m** out of the wall, every second one sheathed in horn.
6. **The crest.** The village's highest roof carries **an Aurochs skull with its horns**, on a post, facing the gate (east), and the
   Hawrog's poles if the village has made one.
7. **Parapets** at every terrace edge, 0.6-0.9 m, with **mammoth ribs** set upright in them as a spiked palisade on the outer faces.
8. **Below the rock:** a **walled pen** (*korl*), its wall of stone and mammoth bones (the ribs and jaws stacked as at Mezhyrich), and
   one or more **round rooms** (*Rawnd*, the kiva's place), half sunk, entered by a ladder through the roof, the roof a
   **glyptodont shell** dome on a stone ring. **Dung cakes** are stacked against the plinth's foot in walls of their own.
9. **Hide screens** on hatches and doors, painted; **drying racks** of bone and pole on the terraces, hung with meat and hides.
10. **What the pueblo never has:** glass, metal roofing, round towers taller than the blocks, any Ancient material (it is the
    dead's: `LORE.md` 6.24).

### Masks (*masg*): the heroes'

1. **A helmet mask**, after the Hopi case mask: a **cylinder of thick rawhide** (aurochs) that covers the whole head, 35-45 cm tall,
   on the bearer's shoulders. Never a face-only mask.
2. **The face is painted, not carved**, on a flat field: kaolin white for the heroes, the First Keepers white with turquoise; the
   eyes are **bars, slots or squares** in black, the mouth a **tube, a beak of horn or a row of teeth**. The hero's **sign** is on the
   brow.
3. **The crown carries the takings**, so a mask is legible as a list of deeds (the table in section 4). Aurochs horns for a Hawrog;
   tusks for Dwsgr; sabre canines for Hemr; antler for Helg; a rhino horn on the brow for Rainw; wolf teeth for Ddair.
4. **A ruff** at the neck of mammoth wool or dire-wolf fur (the Hopi spruce collar's place), dyed or plain.
5. **A finger bone** of the hero sewn into the brow, under a small square of turquoise or ivory.
6. **Silhouette rule:** a hero must read at 50 m by its crown alone. Two heroes on one stage never share a crown shape.
7. **Worn by men, carried by women** (the owner, 2026-11-11). A mask is drawn on a man only in a dance; everywhere else it is a
   **hide bundle** on a woman's back (a tied roll of painted hide, 50-60 cm, the crown's horns or tusks standing out of its top),
   or hung in the Mask House. A kit never draws a man walking with a mask in his hands.
8. **The First Keepers** (the Ancients, the Hall's own: the owner, 2026-11-11) are the only masks with a scrap of the Hall's white
   Ancient metal on them, beside their turquoise.
9. **Heroes, not spirits.** Dhelv's kiva carries katsina-style spirits (`kits/zeijani/PLAN.md` 6.2); a Kimr mask is always a person
   who lived, so it carries that person's takings and sign, never a nature figure (no clouds, corn or birds as beings).
10. **The Bogi** (Two-Snout ogres): black and red, **two hide trunks** hanging from one broad snout, little eyes, a strip of real
   Two-Snout hide; the only masks with a face that is not flat.
11. **The clowns** (*llawn*): no mask; bodies painted in **ochre and white stripes**, gourd caps with knobs; the Melon Monk in a
   saffron-dyed hide and a gourd hat.

### Dolls (*dol Hirw*)

Poplar or larch root (ivory for a Hawrog), **15-40 cm**, blocky and frontal, arms carved against the body, painted exactly as the
mask, with a real scrap of the hero's takings tied on. Hung from the rafters of every house in rows.

### Regalia and dress

1. **The mantle**: one whole hide over the shoulders, fur in, its painted side out (aurochs for the Hawrog, tusker for the line,
   wolf for the sworn); the shoulder line is wide and humped: the Kimr silhouette.
2. **Kilts and sashes** woven of mammoth wool, after the Pueblo kilt and sash: undyed, with a **border band** of the stepped
   terrace and the herd row (section 5) in black, red and white.
3. **Bare arms and chests** on warriors, scarred, the scars rubbed red; leggings and moccasin-boots of hide with the fur out in winter.
4. **Jewellery is the takings**: wolf-tooth necklaces (one per bond), claw charms, ivory ear spools, elk-tooth rows sewn on robes.
   Turquoise only on the Mask-keeper and the First Keepers' masks.
5. **Hair** long, bound with sinew and bone tubes; the Hemr shave the sides.

### Armour and weapons

| Piece | Material | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurochs-horn helm** | the bull's own frontal bone, horns on, lined with hide | horns raised and forward, spread **1.4-1.8 m** on the great helms: the largest silhouette in the culture. Only the Hawrog |
| skull helms | sabre-tooth skull (the Hemr), rhino skull with horn (Rainw), wolf skull | the skull sits on the brow, its jaw cut away |
| antler crown | elk antler, one tine | scouts and runners |
| **tusker-hide cuirass** | layered tusker hide 2-3 cm thick, wrinkled, laced at the sides | the line warriors (Dwsgr); left its natural grey-brown, painted only at the chest |
| scale shirt | glyptodont osteoderms, each a rosette plate 5-8 cm, sewn on hide | the guards of the Hed |
| **round shield** | tusker hide on a bone hoop, 0.7-0.9 m, or a glyptodont shell segment | painted with the owner's sign: a centred figure inside rings (Pueblo shields) |
| heavy spear | 3 m larch shaft, ivory or obsidian blade 30-40 cm, a crossbar of horn below it | the megafauna spear; every hunter |
| spear-thrower and darts | horn thrower, 1.5 m darts | the hunt's main arm |
| war club | a stone head on a femur, or a mammoth femur alone | the commonest weapon |
| glyptodont tail mace | the spiked tail club of *Doedicurus*, on a haft | rare: a chief's |
| sabre-tooth dagger | a canine, 25-30 cm, hilted in ivory | carried by every Hemr |
| **ivory greatsword** (*bleid*) | a split mammoth tusk, 1.3-1.6 m, with obsidian teeth set along both edges | carried by the Hawrog's band: the Cimmerian sword, made of the beast |

**No metal weapons** but a few traded copper knives and Fellgang salvage, which a kit draws as rare and prized. The Ancient metal in
the Hall is never carried (nogo).

### Pottery

1. **Black-on-white bowls** (after Mimbres) for the heroes and the dead: a kaolin slip, a **central figure** (an animal, a hero, a
   hunt) inside a ring, and **rim bands** of stepped and zigzag geometry; the line is fine and black.
2. **The kill hole.** A burial bowl has a hole knocked through the centre of its figure before it goes over the dead's face: every
   bowl in the Hall's niches has one.
3. **Ollas** (water jars) after Acoma: thin-walled, globular, white with black and red stepped terraces and the herd row.
4. **Red-on-buff** bowls for everyday; **corrugated grey** cooking pots, sooty (`ceramic.terracotta.sooty`).
5. **Horn cups** for melon beer, and **gourd** and **glyptodont-shell** vessels for storage.

### Painting: walls, hides, rock

1. **The Rawnd's murals** (the round rooms): bands of **masked heroes in procession**, flat colour on a plaster ground, the figures
   frontal and stiff, after the Pueblo kiva murals; the herd row below them.
2. **Hide paintings** on mantles, screens and shields: the owner's sign and their takings as small figures.
3. **Ochre hand-prints** on the Hall's niches (one per mourner), and on a house's wall for each child born in it.
4. **Rock art** on the paddocks' walls: the herds, the hunts, the labyrinth sign, pecked and painted, at every corridor's mouth.
5. **The Count's tallies**: rows of black strokes in fives, grouped by beast sign, on the Lookout's walls and on hide.

### Hide, horn and bone (the material rules)

- **Every part of every animal**: a Kimr scene has no wasted material. A kit dressing a village draws hides, horns, bones and dung as
  stores and building stuff, not as litter.
- **Hide** is for mantles, screens, shields, drums, boats (round bull-boats on the lakes), litters and the masks; rawhide for the
  masks and shields, tanned for clothes.
- **Horn** caps ladder poles and vigas, makes cups, spoons, bows, spear crossbars and the helms.
- **Bone** frames: mammoth bones for vigas, pens and fences; ribs for palisades; shoulder blades for shovels and drums' rims.
- **Ivory** for blades, spools, dolls and the greatsword: the heroes' material.
- **Skulls** go up: on the crest, on poles at the Hall, over the niches. A kit never lays a skull on the ground.
- **Shell** (glyptodont) roofs the round rooms, covers cisterns, shields, and is cut for bowls.

## 5. Motifs

| Motif | Shape | Means | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the labyrinth** | the classical seven-ring labyrinth, square or round (the Hopi *tapu'at*) | **the Range**: the Kimr's whole world, and the way in and out | the emblem; hatch frames of the Mask-keeper's house; rock art at every corridor's mouth |
| **the Aurochs's head** | frontal, horns raised into a lyre, a dot for each eye | kingship; the Boss's beast | the crest; the Hawrog's things; the emblem over the labyrinth |
| **the stepped terrace** | a stepped triangle, the pueblo's and the mesas' profile (the Pueblo cloud terrace) | the village; the mesas; rain | kilt bands, ollas, parapet tops, the masks' crowns (as a board) |
| **the herd row** | a band of small animals walking one way, each a few strokes | the Count; plenty | kilt bands, ollas, murals' bases |
| **the hand** | an ochre hand-print, fingers spread | the mourner's mark; a birth | the Hall's niches; house walls |
| **the tally** | strokes in fives under a beast's sign | the Count | the Lookout; hide records |
| **the takings** | a horn, a tusk, a fang, an antler, as small signs | the deeds | masks' crowns, mantles, a person's shield |
| **the two trunks** | two curved strokes from one stem | the Two-Snout: danger, nogo | only on the Bogi masks and on warning cairns at a Two-Snout family's ground |
| **the kill hole** | a ragged hole through a figure's centre | the dead | burial bowls |

**Never:** the spiral (the Talovolci's), knotwork, script of any kind, the Ancients' stencilled signs copied as decoration (they are
the dead's).

## 7. Checks a kit runs (when one is built)

- **Silhouette:** each mask in a set has a distinct crown outline (an outline-overlap check on the masks' side views).
- **No ground doors:** no pueblo room's door has a sill under 2.4 m over its plinth's top.
- **Ladder poles clear the hatch** by 1.5 m or more.
- **Skulls up:** no skull record sits under 1.5 m.
- **No Ancient material** in any Kimr building's material list.
- **Every part:** a village's stores record a use for each beast's parts (hide, horn, bone, dung, fat), from `kits/fauna`'s carcass parts.
