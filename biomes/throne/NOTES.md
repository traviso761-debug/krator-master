# The Throne — notes

The volcano at the centre of the crater, and the island it makes in the Ring Sea. Design notes only: no kit yet.
The scale model's biome region is 'Throne/Volcano' (about 150 to 190 km out from the vent).

## The brief (the owner, 2026-10-06)

- Geysers, lava tubes, ash storms and lava. **Ash is fairly frequent.**
- The mountain runs the gamut of climates: it is tall, and its rain shadow is strong.
- Between the rain and the ash it is **very fertile, and very dangerous** to live on.
- **The windward side is the hyperjungle**, more or less: Girder-height trees or lower (it blows up every so often),
  with some local exotics.
- **Going round to the lee, the land turns alien.** Bioluminescent, fungiform extremophiles take over, until it is at
  least as alien as the Rift. Krator's familiar species (frill-trees, trumpet trees) appear on the way. Beyond a point
  little of the life is familiar from Earth, or is even plant life. Perhaps killer slime molds.
- **Down there it is an alien world.** Only the hardiest Earth life holds on: rats, roaches and lichen are everywhere.
- **Zey'danin faces Voth, on the south-west.** That side is the bleeding edge: Vothic colonists try to establish spice
  plantations, and the natives fight back with poison-tipped arrows.
- **People.** Voth is gaining on the disunited Hykkousoi colonies and has taken Zey'danin. The natives hate both and
  want them gone. Some tribes are related to the Ring Sea Islanders; there are stranger tribes on the mountain too.
- **The spice tree is symbiotic with a fungus that thrives only in limited and frustratingly enigmatic conditions**
  (the owner, 2026-10-06, replacing "only on the Throne and the inner isles": there is a canonical **Spice Coast**
  farther away). The Throne's vent country is the richest and surest ground for it, which is why Voth and the Hykkousoi
  fight over the mountain; but it is not the only place. It is **psychoactive, a stimulant extract or resin**, perhaps fond of acid
  and sulphur, and perhaps it **confers superhuman healing** (among other things), which makes it more than a luxury.
  Not too much like Dune's spice.
- **Maybe:** the Chichani came from the island (not only from under the plume). Driven out of the crater in the
  Cataclysm, they kept a prophecy that they would return.
- **Zey'danin** (the name is not settled) was **founded by natives** and has passed back and forth in the colonial
  games for centuries.

## What the scale model says (4.19)

The vent is at map px (575, 504). The summit is at 10.9 km, on a summit plateau about 50 km across above 9 km, with a
caldera floor at about 10.3 km. The shore is the Ring Sea at about −1.5 km. That is 12.4 km of relief over a radius of
about 125 km: a shield, not a cone. The island is about 55,000 km² (within 140 km of the vent) and carries 25 of the
model's climate classes. The land bridge to Spice isle leaves it on the west.

The wind is from the north-west. Windward flanks take 2–4 m of rain a year; the lee (east, south-east) takes 0.2–0.25 m.
The south and north-east flanks are in between (Mediterranean and savanna).

| height | windward (N, NW, W, SW) | lee (NE, E, SE, S) |
|---|---|---|
| shore (−1.5 to 0 km, 1.9–1.6 atm, +30°) | XA/Af hyperjungle | Aw / BSh / BWh |
| 0–2.5 km | Cfa, 2–4 m | BSh/BSk |
| 2.5–3.5 km | Cfb (the cloud belt) | Csa/Csb |
| 3.5–5 km | Dfb/Dfc | Dsb/Dsc |
| 5–7 km | ET | ET at ~100 mm |

**Zey'danin** (map px 523, 530) is 116 km from the vent, bearing 244° (west-south-west), 4 km from the shore, about
1 km above the sea. It is in the wettest band (4 m, Af, +27° mean). **Temora** (490, 553) is on Spice isle, 80 km
further on.

## How thin the air gets

The model's formula is 1.6 atm × e^(−h / 8 km), with oxygen at about 21%.

| height | pressure | what it is like |
|---|---|---|
| shore (−1.5 km) | 1.9 atm | the abyss cough |
| 9 km | 0.57 atm | like the highest towns on Earth (La Rinconada, 5.1 km). Nobody lives higher |
| 10.9 km (the Throne's summit) | 0.41 atm | between La Rinconada and the top of Everest: climbable, not liveable |
| 12.5 km | 0.33 atm | the top of Everest: the death zone for the unadapted |
| ~15 km | 0.2 atm | blackout within minutes. The practical meaning of **airless** |
| 17 km (the Godthrone) | 0.19 atm | blackout in about a minute |
| 26 km | 0.06 atm | blood boils (the Armstrong limit) |

The model's class O starts at 9 km. On the Throne that band is **the death zone**, not airless. "Airless" stays the
word for the Godthrone and the Outer Wall. At 0.75 g the true scale height would be nearer 11 km than 8, so the
model's figures are the cautious ones.

## Why the lee is alien

The plume leans south-east with the wind, day after day, over the same ground. That makes the lee:

1. **Dim.** It lives in the plume's haze, a long twilight. Photosynthesis does worst there; fungi and decomposers do
   best. Glow pays when it is dim (to call spore carriers and pollinators), so the bioluminescence has a reason.
2. **Buried.** Fresh ash keeps falling. Rooted plants lose. What wins grows up from below (mycelium sending up fruiting
   bodies) or rides on top (rosettes with long runners over the ash).
3. **Acid.** The plume's acid fog kills leaves (Masaya, in Nicaragua, has a dead zone downwind).
4. **Fed by the volcano.** The plume is mostly steam, so the lee gets little rain but a steady acid drizzle. The vents
   give off sulphur, hydrogen sulphide and hydrogen, which microbes live on without light.

**Canon (the owner): it is Krator's own life.** A tidally heated moon of a gas giant (Io's case) is the place for
native life to have started round volcanic vents. Earth's life spread over most of Krator; under the plume the native
biosphere holds out, and the deeper under the plume, the less Earth there is. Of Earth's life only the hardiest and most
adaptable get in: rats, roaches and lichen everywhere, and some hardy grasses and birds (the owner, 2026-10-06).

## Rift zones: why the lee is vent country and the south-west is not

A shield's flank eruptions come from a few rift zones, marked by lines of cinder cones, fissures and pit craters.
**The Throne's rifts run north-north-east and south-east**, into the lee. So the fumaroles, hot springs and flank
vents lie mostly under the plume (the native life lives on them), and the south-west sector never opens.

## Zey'danin and the south-west frontier

**Why Zey'danin lasts.** It is crosswind of the plume (the ash falls south-east of the vent; Zey'danin is about 110°
off that line). It is 116 km out, and the longest lava flows on record run about 50–65 km. No rift points at it.
Lahars still come down the valleys from the glaciers above, so the old city stands on the spurs between them. Its
walls could include lahar walls (Hykkousoi work, now Voth's).

**The frontier.** The south-west flank is the wettest ground on the island (4 m) and faces Voth and Spice isle. That is
where the colonists clear the windward jungle for spice plantations. The real analogue is the Banda Islands: nutmeg
under a volcano, and a colonial company that took them by force (1621).

**The poison.** The natives' arrow poison could come from the lee: the slime mat, the acid-marsh rosettes. The alien
side is their armoury and their refuge, where colonists cannot follow.

**The spice.** Canon (the owner, 2026-10-06): the tree is **symbiotic with a certain fungus that thrives only in limited
and frustratingly enigmatic conditions**. Where the fungus takes, the tree bears; where it does not, the tree grows and
bears nothing, and nobody can say why. The Throne is the best-known ground for it, not the only one: there is a
canonical **Spice Coast** farther away. It is one particular spice, apart from the cacao and other spices of Xanadu. A candidate, built on two real resins:

- **Agarwood (oud)**: the most costly resin on Earth forms only when a fungus infects a wounded *Aquilaria* tree; the
  tree seals the wound with it. **Dragon's blood** (*Croton lechleri*'s red sap) is used to this day to seal wounds.
- **So:** an Earth-descended tree whose wounds are colonised by **its fungus** (on the Throne a native of the acid,
  sulphurous vent ground). The tree answers with a red resin that seals and regrows its own tissue. That is the healing: in people it
  closes wounds and knits bone in days. Chewed raw (the bark or the unripe resin) it is a stimulant, like coca or khat.
- **Where it bears (the enigma):** the fungus's conditions are narrow and not understood. On the Throne it is reliable
  only at the seam, the rift edges, the shoulders and the isles' geyser ground; elsewhere it takes in a few places and
  not their neighbours (the Spice Coast is one such place). Growers argue over soil, ash, sulphur, mist, the tree's age,
  even prayer; nobody can make it take on purpose. A candidate for what is going on (not canon): the fungus needs
  something of Krator's own biosphere that persists only patchily outside the plume.
- **Why it is fought over:** soldiers who heal in days. That is strategic, not a luxury.
- **The cost (not Dune's addiction or prescience):** too much and the body heals too much, like the tree's burls:
  proud flesh, knotted scars, growths. Old users show it.
- **Wild against planted:** on the south-west flank the fungus has not taken in the colonists' plantations, for no
  reason anyone can find (they cart ash and sulphur to the groves, with little success). The best resin is wild, in native-held vent country.
  That drives the raids, and the arrows.
- **One tree, two uses (a candidate):** the same tree's sap, prepared another way, is the natives' arrow poison.

**A hook for Tzintzun**, who does not seem to age: the rumour that it is the spice. The Monks of History suspect the
life-transfer devices (LORE.md §6.12). The spice is the cover story, or part of the truth.

## Around the mountain

Working names. The numbers are the reference board (`refs/`, below).

**Windward hyperjungle (NW, W, SW).** The hyperjungle's own species. **Tree height follows the age of the ground**:
Girder's 150–270 m only on the shore band (1.9 atm) and on the oldest kīpuka (islands of old forest left between
younger lava flows). Elsewhere 60–120 m, because a flow or a lahar resets the ground every few centuries. Local
exotics: arch palms and red bromeliads on red laterite (8); pink scale cones in the fern gullies cut into the lava
(18); glowing brackets only where it is dark, in tube mouths and ravines (6); columnar basalt in the gorges and on the
coast (3).

**The shoulders (N and S flanks, turning dry).** Krator's familiar species cross in here: frill-trees (crater
drylands), cap-trees (SW Bay), trumpet trees (the Rift, N highlands). Star aloes over yellow grass on the south
flank's savanna, with grazers (13); cap-tree woodland (10); stilt parasols along the braided lahar channels (11);
gill-coral trees among conifers in the snow of the cold belt (2).

**Under the plume (E, SE).** Ash creepers: rosettes on long runners over the ash (15); pagoda caps on the ash desert
(12); the red plume-bush, with ember urchins (spiky, ember-eyed critters) in the grass (14); drizzle trumpets, the
lee's cousin of the trumpet tree, catching the plume's drizzle (19); soot cups and stalk daisies on striped rhyolite
hills (5).

**Vent country (along the rifts, densest under the plume).** The sulphur marsh with its antler-stalked rosettes (4);
the mat (7); vent coral (16) and brain caps (17) round hot pools on black lava; bone bells in the steam valleys,
bleached by the acid (1); puffball rope-trees in the high cold mist (9); lamp caps glowing at night (20).

**The high cold (both sides).** Windward: glaciers, glacier bursts and lahars down the wet flank; ice towers and ice
caves over the summit's fumaroles (Mt Erebus), warm living spots at −30°. Lee: penitentes, fields of snow blades (as on
the dry Andes volcanoes).

### The slime molds

Real slime molds move a few centimetres an hour, so not a hunter. Two believable versions:
- **The mat**: it takes over a lava-tube floor or a hollow in a night and digests whatever sleeps on it.
- **The hollows**: CO₂ pools in still hollows and kills (Lake Nyos, Mammoth Mountain); the mat eats the carcasses and
  keeps the hollow clean. Worse to stumble into than a predator.

## Volcanic features

- **Lava flows of every age (the kit's spine).** Like the crater drylands' fire mosaic, with flows instead of burns.
  Each flow has an age. On the wet side, fern and forest come back in decades and leave kīpuka. On the lee, flows stay
  black for centuries.
- **Lava tubes**: fern gardens in the skylights (collapsed roofs), roots hanging into them, ice all year in the high
  ones. Shelter for people.
- **Ash**: falls south-east. Ash storms, ash dunes, wind-carved ash ridges on the lee. Hawaii's Kaʻū Desert formed the
  same way (rain shadow plus fallout).
- **Geysers** belong on the wet side, where a rift meets groundwater; the model's ten geysers are on islets 155–175 km
  offshore. Fumaroles, yellow sulphur crusts (blue sulphur flames at night, as at Ijen), acid crater lakes, mud pots,
  sinter: along the rifts.
- **Rift zones**: lines of cinder cones (forested, bare or snow-capped by belt), pit craters, fissure curtains of fire.
- **The coast**: black and olivine-green sand, columnar basalt, steam where lava enters the sea, pumice rafts.
- **Lava casts of hypertrees**: a flow into the hyperjungle leaves hollow stone casts of the giants, tens of metres
  across.
- **Hazards**: ash storms, acid fog, the CO₂ hollows, lightning in the plume, lahars, glacier bursts, the summit glow.

## Fauna hooks

Birds that bury their eggs in volcanically warm ground (the maleo); blind glowing cave life in the tubes; sky rays on
the plume's thermals; the ember urchins (14). Rats and roaches everywhere.

the pit crater with its acid lake, the steaming two-year cone, the Lower cone), eight lava flows from 2 to 5,200 years old

map from north to south: ash pines, frill-trees, trumpet trees and star aloes in the west; gill-parasols, stilt parasols
and the wild spice trees on the seam; pagoda caps, drizzle trumpets, rope-trees and lamp caps under the plume; bone bells
in the steam. Day and night (N): at night Krator's own life glows. The owner's 32 textures of the same day are in the
library and the kit (`core/materials/PLAN.md`, The Throne).

**Station 2, the kipuka** (`dist/throne-kipuka.html`, `python3 build.py --station kipuka`; `stations/kipuka/`): a 5.2 km
piece of the windward west flank, ~110 km from the summit, ~300 m below the datum, ~4 m of rain. Five flows from 3 to
2,600 years old have run down the flank round the old hills; what they spared for ~1,700 years is **kipuka**, and the
**hyperjungle kit** plants it (two kits on one page, as `biomes/WORLD.md` plans: read in place from `biomes/hyperjungle`).
The Throne kit plants the rest, "basically normal, a little alien flavour" (the owner): **great ruffs** on the kipuka's
rims (first made as a "hyper frill-tree"; the owner kept the look under another name: collars of spiked fronds that burst
in a flow's heat and seed the young lava round it: the **ruff trees** there), the **frill tree** (the Rift's: a ribbed
column finned all the way up, the owner's fractal fronds as its fins), **lehua** and **tree ferns** taking the young lava, **arch palms** with
red bromeliads, the **glassfern** first on the fresh rock, **lava casts** of the trees the flows swallowed at the kipuka's

**Station 3, the spice frontier** (`dist/throne-frontier.html`, `python3 build.py --station frontier`; `stations/frontier/`):

**no resin** (the fungus has not taken in them: the plantations fail, NOTES above), stone-faced terrace risers, a cart **road**
up the spur, two **fresh clearings** of burnt slash and sawn hypertree stumps. The natives' **foot trails** come out of the
forest to the fields' edges, where they set **snares** and **punji pits** (records: `FRONTIER.traps`, culture
`throne-natives`), and tend **angel trumpets** and **spider lilies** (the arrow poisons) along the forest's margin. The sea
is the shared wave field (`core/atmos`).

**Station 4, the geyser isle** (`dist/throne-isle.html`, `python3 build.py --station isle`; `stations/isle/`): one of the
inner isles, ~170 km west of the summit (the mountain low on the eastern horizon). The scale model's isles are 20-35 km
domes; this is one of the small ones (ideal type, ~4 km across, 230 m high) so the whole isle and its shores fit the page
(the owner: "we're gonna be on the beach"). Its **crown** holds the geyser basin in a shallow hollow on the rift that

them. Round it the **warm ground**, where the spice's fungus takes and the wild spice bears; the natives' **resin camp**
(lean-tos, a hearth, resin cakes curing on a rack, the nearest spice trees tapped into gourds: `ISLE`, culture
`throne-natives`), their **canoes** at the landing on the west beach, a trail between. Down the slopes **an island's
forest**, smaller than the flank's: lehua over tree ferns, palm frill trees; gullies with streams, one falling over the
**sea cliffs** on the windward north-west. **Black-sand beaches** under **coconut palms** (the owner: an actual coconut,
in the fruit system: `generic_fruit_coconut`) and **palm frill trees** (the owner: "a palm frill tree with super wide
upper frills"), wrack along the high-water line; the **warm lagoon** in the lee, where the basin's creek comes out, with
the **hyper-mangroves** (the owner: "some sort of hyper-mangrove") on their prop roots; **kelp** 3-15 m down round the
isle. The sea is the shared wave field.

**Station 5, the cloud forest** (`dist/throne-cloud.html`, `python3 build.py --station cloudforest`; `stations/cloudforest/`):
the windward cloud belt ~3 km up the west flank (Cfb, the cloud most days; the owner: "elfin forest in the mist"). The
**elfin woods**: gnarled, dwarfed trees of two to four crooked stems, every limb cushioned with moss and hung with moss
curtains, bromeliads in the forks, flat crowns shorn by the wind; big tree ferns; a floor of moss and green ferns. The
**veil trees** are Krator's own (the little alien flavour): pale slender trees whose arching limbs hang veils of fine pink
filaments that comb the water from the cloud, glowing faintly at night. **The ravine of the falls** steps down the flank
in four falls (20-37 m) into plunge pools between mossy walls; two gentler ravines with streams. An old **rift ridge**
runs along the south edge: the cloud pours over its crest and thins away over the **drier heath** in its lee (ash pines:
the dry side's species, read from the 'humid' field). The natives' **trail** climbs to the ridge's **saddle**, marked
by cairns, with a great cairn at the saddle (a carved post, offerings of resin). The mist is the shared atmosphere's
(`core/atmos`: fog banks, the weather: fog, drizzle, a clear spell).

**Station 6, the south-flank savanna** (`dist/throne-savanna.html`, `python3 build.py --station savanna`;
`stations/savanna/`): the south shoulder ~1 km up in the dry season (Aw/BSh). **Tall yellow grass** (the library's dry
grass tussocks, thick near the cameras) with **star aloes**, and Krator's familiar crossers scattered through it, the
**frill-trees** and **trumpet trees**; **gill-parasol woods** on the older ground. Down the middle a **lahar fan** widening
south: grey gravel bars between **braided channels**, dry now but for their pools, **stilt parasols** along them, and
**lahar boulders** the size of huts strewn up-fan. A **fresh burn** east of the fan: black stubble, the first green and
the fire flowers. **Kopjes** of old lichened basalt and two **dongas** (dry gullies). No people and no grazers yet (the
fauna kit; the life layer).

**Station 7, deep under the plume** (`dist/throne-ash.html`, `python3 build.py --station ashdesert`; `stations/ashdesert/`):
the ash desert ~2.2 km up the south-east flank, ~65 km from the vent, right under the plume's axis: its **ceiling
overhead**, ash falling most days. Krator's own life holds it: **pagoda caps** standing in the ash, ash creepers, **drizzle
trumpets**, rope-trees (thinning out where the ash lies deep), **lamp caps** glowing at night, soot cups and stalk daisies
on three **striped rhyolite hills**; of Earth's life only the hardiest (rats, roaches, lichen, hardy grasses, birds).
**Ash dunes** across the wind in the south-east half, **yardangs** carved from cemented ash in the north-west, two old
flows half buried and scoured dark, and a **CO2 hollow**: the mat on its floor, nothing else alive in it, bones on its rim.
The weather is the shared atmosphere's new opt-in **ash weather** (`core/atmos`, from Voth's ash storm, the owner asked
for it): `ashfall` most days, and the **ash storm**, a gale of flecks and streaks of driven ash, veils racing along the
ground, a brown-out, warm lightning; the module's other modes besides.

**Station 8, vent country** (`dist/throne-vents.html`, `python3 build.py --station vents`; `stations/vents/`): the east
rift ~1.4 km up, ~80 km from the vent, under the plume. A rift zone is a broad ridge radial to the summit, and the dykes
that feed its fissures have wedged its crest apart into a **graben**: its floor dropped ~20-40 m between two fault scarps
~470 m apart, open cracks (gjár) en echelon on it, steaming. Up-rift an **eruptive fissure** four years quiet: spatter
ramparts either side of its crack, four spatter cones along it, its young flow down the graben floor, and the crack still
glowing orange at night; nothing lives on its hot ramparts or its cones (the owner's rule: the host's mask and `barren`). Down-rift the **sulphur marsh**: acid water among crusted hummocks, its
antler-stalked rosettes, held by a bank. On the east shoulder's black lava the **hot pools**, each ringed by its own
mats (blue where hottest, then green, yellow, orange as it cools, streaming out down the runoff), vent coral and brain
caps round them, and **mud pots** beside them, bubbling. In the west shoulder a **steam valley**, its rock rotted to
white clay stained ochre and rust, fumaroles all down its floor and bone bells standing in the steam. Two still
**hollows** where the CO2 pools, the mat on their floors, bones on one's rim. Away from the steam the acid rain and the
gas keep the shoulders sparse: the life crowds the vents. On all the sulphurous ground (the marsh, the vents' crusts, the
cracks' lips, the steam valley) the **sulphur life** the owner asked for, Krator's own extremophiles living on the vents'
gas and crusted with the sulphur they make: **brimstone candelabras** on stilt roots in the acid shallows, their banded
arms turning up to fluted orange cups that glow faintly at night; **brimstone reeds** in stands, rust below, sulphur
above, crusted tassels; **acid pads** floating on the open water; clusters of **gas bladders** that glow at night. The weather is the shared atmosphere's: its fog is the rift's
**acid fog** here (yellowed, the sun a pale disc), with the opt-in ash besides.

**Station 9, the glacier and its ice caves** (`dist/throne-glacier.html`, `python3 build.py --station glacier`;
`stations/glacier/`): the north-west flank ~5.6-6.5 km up, ~60 km from the vent, the summit plateau's snowfield filling the
south-east of the sky and the windward cloud belt lying far below as a sea of cloud. The flank comes down in steps (an upper
basin, an icefall, a long slope, a bench, then the fall below it), and a **valley glacier** fills it: snow on its basin,
the **icefall**'s seracs and crevasses, bare ice below with dirt bands (ogives), a medial moraine and dirty margins, ending
on the bench in an **ice cliff** calving into a milky **proglacial lake** behind the **terminal moraine**; lateral moraines
along both sides. The **meltwater river** leaves through the moraine's breach and braids down the flank on its own graded
line (it never runs uphill: a probe holds it to that). Low down the **cold belt** (refs/02): **gill-coral trees** (new:
twisted stems streaked teal and dark red, broad salmon caps ridged like plate coral, tiered on the big ones) among conifers
dusted with snow, teal cushions and fallen caps on the snow; the gill-corals are riparian (the owner's), crowding the river's
banks while the conifers keep back from the water; and at the treeline the **glass willow** (new, Krator's own): limbs arching
out and down, hung with long translucent ice-blue strands and icicles. Beside the ice a **geothermal shelf** (Mt Erebus): fumaroles,
**ice towers** of frozen steam round them, three **ice caves** the steam has hollowed (lit blue through their walls; warm
inside, where Krator's own life lives at -30 outside: the mat, moss, glow mushrooms, brain caps, lamp caps) and an **ice
arch**. The weather is the shared atmosphere's new opt-in **snow** (`core/atmos`, the same flakes as the ash): `snowfall`
and the **blizzard** (a gale of flakes, streaks of blown snow, spindrift on the ice, a white-out).

**Station 10, the rim over the caldera** (`dist/throne-caldera.html`, `python3 build.py --station caldera`;
`stations/caldera/`): the summit, the caldera's west rim ~10.75 km up, ~0.4 atm (the death zone: climbable, not
liveable). Thin air: a hard white sun, a deep blue sky going toward indigo overhead, almost no haze, the cloud belts lying
far below on every side. The rim's peaks are armoured in ice on their outer side, with **frozen fumaroles** (ice towers,
steaming) on the crest and sulphur round them; inside, the **wall** drops in terraces ~450 m, banded by its old flows, to
the **floor** of old lava with six spatter cones, and in the floor the **lava lake**: its crust of dark plates cracking and
turning over on the glowing lava, glowing veins round its pit, **the plume** roaring up out of it (a column of billows
kilometres high, leaning away south-east, lit orange from below at night: the **summit glow**). Behind the rim the summit
plateau's snowfield is carved by the sun into **penitentes**, close-packed blades of hard snow in rows. Nothing lives but
lichen on the rocks and moss and the mat on the warm ground by the fumaroles (the kit's `deathzone` field). The weather is
the shared atmosphere's, both opt-ins at once: snow (and the blizzard off the rim) and ash (when the plume leans this way).
The **Eruption** toggle (the owner's; a button, and two presets): lava fountains out of four vents in the lake and bombs
thrown over the floor, the lake's crust breaking up and churning bright, the plume rising twice as fast, swelling and
darkening with ash, the glow flaring, ash falling and lightning in the plume; it eases in over ~6 s and dies away over
~25 s. Realism pass (2026-10-06): the wall's rock triplanar (no smearing down its 450 m), the plume's billows lit as
spheres by the sun with noise-broken edges, the lake's crust the library's aa lava, glints of the sun on the snow; then the lake itself (the owner's): a lobed, ragged
outline (`LAKE.rAt`) shared by its pit, its fields and its glowing ring, a surface that heaves in 3D (swells in its vertex
shader, domed over the vents in an eruption), and a crust in two scales, torn plates (a noise-warped Voronoi) with glowing
cracks broken into small facets each tilted and shaded on its own, thin glowing seams between them, some thin enough for
the heat to glow through, and the
snow and ice sampled with a finer stochastic tiling (`THRONE.GLSL_LAY`'s `_layd`).
*For Godot:* the eruption's state (`SUMMIT.eruptStep`) is pure and eased, as the shared atmosphere's weather is; the
fountains and bombs are ballistic in their vertex shader (`GPUParticles3D` with gravity); the plume a lit billboard particle
shader (or a `FogVolume`); the triplanar cliff `StandardMaterial3D.uv1_triplanar`; the glints and the crust plain shader
maths; the glow a flickering `OmniLight3D`.

**Station 11, a lava tube** (`dist/throne-tube.html`, `python3 build.py --station tube`; `stations/tube/`): under an old
flow on the south-east shoulder, ~1.6 km up at the plume's edge. The owner's: "a lava tube cavern", old and living with one
hot reach. The tube runs ~1.4 km, falling gently downstream: an arched passage 13-24 m wide and 8-15 m high (a swept mesh,
not the heightfield), a flat floor of old ropy lava, **flow ledges** along both walls, **lavacicles** in drifts on the roof.
Three **skylights** where the roof fell in: daylight down each (a spot light, a shaft of light, motes), a breakdown pile of
the fallen roof below it, and the life that wants light there (siphon trees climbing out of the hole, lamp caps, ferns and
moss; root and moss curtains hanging from the hole's edges; trees leaning over its rim above). Between them, in the dark,
**Krator's own cave life**: glow mushrooms, pale fungi, scale cones and the mat on the floor (the kit's new cave pass),
lantern brackets on the walls, fungi dripping from the roof, glow on the ledges. A **side passage** ends in a choke.
Downstream the **hot reach**: the tube still young and hot, a **lava stream** running in a channel in its floor (a crust
carried downstream in its shader), the walls glazed and glowing near the floor, nothing alive; it ends in a sump. The
visitor carries a dim lamp (in the dark between the skylights the cave would read as black). No weather under the rock.
**Heat shimmer** (the owner's, 2026-10-07; `src/83-host-heat.js`, also over the caldera's lake): sheets over the hot
lava re-read the rendered frame at a rising, wavering offset that fades at their edges, with height and with distance (in
Godot a spatial shader on the same sheets reading `hint_screen_texture` and `hint_depth_texture` at `SCREEN_UV + offset`).
It runs only with the heat in reach of the camera (the frame then goes through an offscreen target); the caldera's
doubles in an eruption.

## Peoples of the island (the owner, 2026-10-07)

Three native cultures, besides the colonists (Voth, the Hykkousoi) on the south-west. **The Cthonians and the ash nomads
may be cousins of the Chichani; the Islanders are ethnically different.** When an unknown past calamity came (in the
Cataclysm, or later), the three kin peoples went three ways: **the ash nomads took to wandering, the Chichani took to the
sea and left the island entirely, and the Cthonians hid.** (So the Chichani's ashen skin and night vision are an old
family trait; Voth's war for the Throne is a homecoming in its own eyes, and the Cthonians are the cousins who stayed.)

**1. The ash nomads and villagers** (cousins of the Cthonians and the Chichani: the ones who took to wandering) herd creatures on the surface: the savanna of the south flank (station 6), the
shoulders and the plume's edge (1), out to the ash desert under the plume (7). Nomads move with the herds and the ash
falls; villagers hold the kopjes, the lahar fans' gravels and the old flows' soil. Their life is the weather: they read the
plume (ashfall, the ash storm), burn the grass for new growth (the burn and its fire flowers), and know the CO2 hollows as
places that kill. Earth's grasses and the hardiest Earth stock live with them; the grazers are the fauna kit's.

**2. The Cthonians** (the owner's spelling; cave dwellers) hid, and are still hidden. Their greatest cities are built in
and around the lava tubes: dwellings carved into the rock, rising as **spires** and sinking as **underground galleries**.
They know the lava tunnels better than anyone, so their scouts and raiding parties appear and disappear without warning.
- **Their places:** one **capital**, main and elaborate (a little smaller than Yuni), *its location unknown to outsiders*;
  **three cave and tufa villages** (each about Mungo's size); a few scattered **surface farming hamlets**.
- **Their arts:** an extensive knowledge of the cave fungi, used in **medicinal and alchemical arts** known only to them;
  and the **alecap**, a peculiar purple mushroom they grow which, fermented, makes a fairly passable beer.
- **Their rock:** the Throne's ash flows, welded where they came to rest, make **ignimbrite (tuff)**: soft enough to carve
  with an adze, hard enough to stand for centuries, eroded by the rain into **fairy chimneys** and cliffed valleys. So their
  country is where the tube fields meet the tuff, and their old lava tubes are their roads, cisterns and refuges.
- **How the caves suit them:** old tubes on dead flows are stable for millennia, cool and constant, with water dripping
  through their roofs and light at the skylights (where they grow the light's life: the siphon trees, the lamp caps, the
  cave ferns). They farm the dark (the glow mushrooms, the pale fungi, the alecap, the mat, carefully); Krator's glowing
  cave life lights their streets.
- **What they fear:** CO2 pooling in low passages (they keep the deep levels' air moving, and carry something that
  sickens before a person does), breakdown (a roof's slow collapse), flash floods (a tube is a drain), and a new eruption on
  the rift that buries a skylight or sends lava down an old tube. Tubes on an active rift are never settled.
- **Large spaces:** lava tubes are long, not roomy (Krator's lower gravity lets roofs span a little more). Their great
  halls are where tubes meet, where breakdown has opened a chamber, a **drained magma chamber** (Þríhnúkagígur, Iceland:
  a bottle 120 m deep), and the halls they cut themselves in the tuff.
- **Vibe (the owner's):** Cappadocia, Petra, Ethiopia's rock-hewn churches, dwarves, Varanasi and Babylon. The reference
  board is `kits/cthonian/refs/` (30 images, 2026-10-07): rock-cut cliff faces and a cavern city, fairy chimneys and
  conical granary towers, glowing pierced temple towers, domed and mushroom-capped houses, cyclopean cube shrines, a
  fortress under an overhang.
- **More to plumb (proposed):** *Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı* (Cappadocia's underground cities: eight levels, rolling stone
  doors, ventilation shafts, stables and chapels below ground); *Vardzia* (Georgia: a cave monastery-city of thirteen levels
  in a cliff) and *Uplistsikhe*; *Ellora's Kailasa* (a temple cut down from the top of a hill, and India's **stepwells**,
  Chand Baori and Rani ki Vav: Varanasi's ghats turned underground, flights of stairs down to cisterns); *Lalibela*'s
  trench churches and *Aksum*'s carved stelae; *the Dogon* (Mali: cliff villages, conical granaries, toguna meeting
  shelters) and the *Tellem* before them; *Mesa Verde* (cliff palaces, kivas); *Matmata* (Tunisia: pit courtyards);
  *Matera*'s Sassi; *Shibam* (Yemen: mud towers); *the Guanches* (the Canaries: a volcanic island's cave people, painted
  caves in tuff; the closest real match); *Rapa Nui*'s and Hawaii's lava-tube refuges and burial caves; *Babylon*'s glazed
  brick and ziggurats; and for the brewing and the fungi, *chicha* (a name that echoes the Chichani), Ethiopian *tella*,
  Japanese *koji*.

**3. The islanders** live on the coasts and the isles and make their living on the water (station 4): fishing, kelp and
seaweed, the coconut and its coir, the reef. Some are related to the Ring Sea Islanders (the brief); they are not kin to the other two. The board's *islander port*
(`kits/cthonian/refs/islander port.jpg`) is theirs: platforms and sail-roofed halls over the water. They trade between
the mountain and the sea, and know the spice's wild groves on the isles' geyser ground; their canoes land on the beaches.

*Next (the owner's):* a kit for the Cthonians first: their carved architecture in tuff and tube, then a settlement and a
station for it (a tuff valley with a carved city, a tube opening in its cliff, a cavern deep inside).
