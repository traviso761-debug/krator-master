# Longsong — the rooms and the outbuildings (the plan before the interiors)

The owner's direction (2026-10-19): the exterior should refer to, and be consistent with, the Order's chapterhouses seen
round Krator, notably their **neo-African** forms; the Sahelian / Timbuktu styling (banco, toron, the Sankore spire, the
Djenné hall) is a Yuni thing the Order does not take up. This file is the plan for (1) that revision of the exterior,
(2) every outbuilding of the grounds, (3) every room of the main mass and the Vault, room by room, before the interiors
are built. `BLUEPRINT.md` is the programme it extends; `PLAN.md` the site and the order of work.

## 1. The Order's own vocabulary, and how Longsong carries it

The Order's buildings at Yuni are built in two languages: Yuni's Sahelian earth (the Hall of Records, the Sankore spire:
**not the Order's**) and the Order's own neo-African modernism, which is what the chapterhouses everywhere share:

| The Order's form (where it is) | What it is | At Longsong |
|---|---|---|
| **The drum compound** (the Chapter house, Obieze-inspired: `settlements/yuni/src/59-civic.js` 2; the ports at Iziz, Ys, Verge) | round halls of red fired brick with a relief band under the eaves, terracotta tile cones carrying an eight-post lantern, a second small cone and a brass ball; round relief-ringed windows; curved two-storey arcaded gallery wings round a circular court; a carved forecourt wall of glyph panels; a pointed-parabolic gate; a blue mosaic reflecting pool; an eight-post rotunda | the **Great Assembly Hall**, the **Lower Gate's paired drums** and the **Hall of Hours** become brick drums under terracotta cones and lanterns (now white under blue cones); the **Debate Court's** galleries become the chapterhouse's curved two-storey brick arcades with parabolic heads; the **Pilgrims' square** becomes the chapterhouse's circular forecourt (glyph-panel wall, reflecting pool, tea rotunda) |
| **The Archive** (CCSE-inspired: 59-civic 1) | an elliptical drum of brick fin-piers with dark recessed glazing between them, a broad wavy (scalloped) pale roof on a timber-slat soffit, a second raised wavy roof, and a brick tower carrying a stack of wavy louvre rings | the **Printing House** in the ward becomes a small Archive (the press that makes the chapterhouses' copies); the **Eastern Watch** becomes the Archive's louvre tower: a brick drum with eight wavy rings, the Order's bell and the instruments in the top ring |
| **The School** (Gaudí's Sagrada Família schools: 59b 14) | undulating brick walls on a whitewashed dado, parabolic heads on the openings, a warped conoid tile roof | the **Hall of Forms** (new: the operatives' drill hall in the ward, beside the drill ground) |
| **The blue-and-white mosaic string course, electric lamps, brass** | the Order's one strong colour, its generator's light | kept everywhere (it is already on the palaces, the gate, the drums) |

**The rule for each building**: the **Tibetan** grammar decides the massing and the climb (battered white base, the Red
Palace over the White, black trapezoid windows, the twig band, flat roofs, gilt roofs, chortens, prayer wheels); the
**Order's** grammar decides every hall that is the Order's own (assembly, debate, gates, archive, press, school, clocks,
the Watch). The valley's lay buildings (the hostel, the servants' houses, the market, the dairy) stay Oidong-Tibetan
vernacular. **Materials**: lime render, fired brick, terracotta tile, granite, timber, brass, gilding; no banco, no
rammed earth, no toron, no pinnacle-buttresses, no Sankore spire (`MCHK.noEarth` checks the plan and the materials).
**Palette**: Tibetan white and red-ochre, the chapterhouses' brick red and terracotta, the Order's blue in mosaic and
glass only (the blue tile cones go back to terracotta), saffron in the cloth.

## 5. The farms and the gardens (what the house eats, wears, burns and writes on)

The owner (2026-10-19): flesh out the farms and gardens. Everything sown here is a crop the Crops kit allows in the
Oidong Valley (`kits/crops`, `CROPS.forBiome('oidong')`: the valley floor and its flats are cool to mild and moist, about
1.24 atm); the page checks it (`FCHK.crops`, with a negative: a field of cotton). The data is `src/40b-ls-farms.js`
(`[G data]`); the drawing `src/85-ls-farms.js`.

**What the house needs.** About 550 live inside the wall (200 monks, 50 lay scholars, 80 operatives, 100 wardens,
120 lay servants) and up to fifty pilgrims on a festival day. Their staple is roasted grain ground to flour (the
valley's tsampa: frost oat, eaten with butter tea): about 180 kg a head a year, some 100 t. At the valley's rain-fed
yields (about 1.4 t a hectare: an assumption) the house's own fields grow about a quarter of it (25 t); the rest comes as the
**tithe of the valley's villages** (the Order holds the estate: the tenants pay in grain, butter, wool and labour at
harvest), and by the caravans. The fields' share is a number in the data (`LS.FARMS.provision()`), not a guess in prose.

## 6. The order of work

1. **The exterior revision** (section 1): the drum compound forms (brick drums, terracotta cones and lanterns, the
   curved brick arcades, the Chapterhouse Court with its glyph wall, pool and rotunda), the Printing House as a small
   Archive, the Eastern Watch as the louvre tower, the Hall of Forms, the new outbuildings (mani wall, chorten garden,
   Garden of Held Water). One screenshot set against the chapterhouses.
2. **The rooms as data** (`src/40-ls-plan.js` or a sibling `[G data]` fragment): every room above a record (id,
   building, level, box, tier, use), so the people's places point at rooms and the interiors have their programme.
3. **The interiors** on `kits/interiors` (ROOM() and the placer, the catalog adapter), level by level from the
   sumptuous rooms down; the furniture records baked headless (README.md, "Furniture that is not always drawn is data
   first"; Mav's Refuge is the worked example).
4. **The textures** (`core/materials/PROMPTS-NEEDED.md`, section "Longsong"): the exterior's first, the interiors'
   before step 3. The first five (the glyph relief, the mosaic band, the red limewash, the prayer wheel's repoussé and
   the mani stones) were delivered 2026-10-19 and are wired.
5. **The farms and gardens** (section 5): the fields, terraces, pools, groves, orchards and beds as records, drawn with
   the library's crop cards; the herds as placeholders until the fauna kit has a yak.
